## A first discharge

Ten lines, including comments and blanks:

```python
import voltaire as vt

# A parameterised 1-D full cell: graphite | separator | NMC.
cell = vt.Cell.from_preset("graphite-nmc-1d")

# Constant-current discharge at 1C for one hour of simulated time.
result = cell.discharge(c_rate=1.0, t_end="1h")

# Voltage against discharged capacity.
result.plot("voltage")
```

### What each line does

`import voltaire as vt` loads the package. Everything user-facing hangs off the top level; `vt.Cell` is the object you will spend most of your time with.

`vt.Cell.from_preset("graphite-nmc-1d")` builds a one-dimensional cell sandwich — porous graphite negative electrode, separator, porous NMC positive electrode — with a built-in parameter set: geometry, transport and kinetic parameters, and open-circuit potential curves for both electrodes. Presets are starting points, not fixed configurations; individual parameters can be overridden before running.

`cell.discharge(c_rate=1.0, t_end="1h")` runs a galvanostatic discharge. `c_rate=1.0` sets the current to drain the nominal capacity in one hour, and `t_end="1h"` sets the simulated duration, so this is nominally a full discharge. Internally, each implicit time step is one damped Newton solve of the full nonlinear residual, using the autodiff Jacobian $J = \partial R / \partial u$ — the same matrix the impedance and sensitivity machinery reuse below.

`result.plot("voltage")` opens a matplotlib figure of cell voltage against discharged capacity. Expect the shape every battery engineer knows: the curve starts near the full-charge open-circuit voltage, drops immediately by the ohmic and kinetic overpotential the 1C current forces, follows a slowly declining plateau whose shape is set by the two electrodes' open-circuit potential curves, and bends down steeply near the end as the graphite empties and transport limits bite. For this chemistry the curve lives roughly in the 4.2 V to 3.0 V window. No numbers are quoted here because none have been produced by a released solver yet.

## An impedance spectrum

With the same cell object at its current operating point:

```python
spec = cell.impedance(f_min=0.01, f_max=1e5, n=50)
spec.plot("nyquist")
```

### What each line does

`cell.impedance(f_min=0.01, f_max=1e5, n=50)` linearises the cell about its present state and solves the frequency-domain system $(i\omega M - J)\hat{u} = \hat{b}$ at 50 logarithmically spaced frequencies from 10 mHz to 100 kHz. There is no time stepping and no simulated sinusoidal forcing: one sparse complex linear solve per frequency, reusing the Jacobian $J$ the Newton solver already assembled. That reuse is the reason impedance is a two-line afterthought here rather than a separate campaign of long time-domain runs.

`spec.plot("nyquist")` draws the standard Nyquist plot, negative imaginary impedance against real impedance. Expect the canonical geometry: a high-frequency intercept on the real axis set by the ohmic resistance of electrolyte and contacts, a depressed arc produced by charge-transfer resistance in parallel with double-layer capacitance at the interfaces, and a Warburg tail rising at roughly 45 degrees at low frequency, where diffusion takes over from kinetics.

The physics behind each feature of that arc is on the [impedance page](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/physics/impedance/); the kinetic and transport models the time-domain solve integrates are on the [kinetics](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/physics/kinetics/) and [transport](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/physics/transport/) pages.

You do not have to wait for the package to poke at the methods. The [impedance demo](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/demos/#demo-impedance) fits a distribution of relaxation times to spectra in your browser, and the [Butler–Volmer demo](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/demos/#demo-butler-volmer) is the kinetic law the discharge solve calls at every interface, live under sliders. ## Caveats

- Function names, argument names, and defaults may change before release; presets may be renamed.
- No output of this API has been benchmarked against other solvers yet. In particular, the comparison against a PyBaMM P2D reference has not yet been run — see [validation](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/validation/) for the current state of checking.
- When behaviour on this page and behaviour of the released package differ, trust the package and report the page: repaka.ashrith@gmail.com.
