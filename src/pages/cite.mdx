# Cite

If Voltaire's methods, its [browser demos](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/demos/), or its write-ups contributed to your work, cite the software. One honest caveat first: the Python solver package is planned and not yet written. What exists today is this site and its in-browser demos, which implement the numerics in TypeScript. <RepoNote kind="citation" />

## BibTeX

```bibtex
@software{repaka_voltaire_2026,
  author  = {Repaka, Ashrith},
  title   = {Voltaire: a multiphysics simulator for electrochemical cells},
  year    = {2026},
  url     = {https://godtier812.github.io/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/},
  license = {MIT}
}
```

A `CITATION.cff` file ships alongside the source and carries the same metadata, so once the repository is public GitHub's "Cite this repository" button will produce this entry in BibTeX or APA form without any copying from this page. Until then, the block above is the canonical form.

## Licence

Voltaire is released under the MIT licence: you may use, copy, modify, and redistribute the code, commercially or otherwise, as long as the copyright and licence notice stay with it. The full text is in `LICENSE`, which ships with the source.

The same terms cover the visual material — the [gallery](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/gallery/) figures, the demo posters, and the plots the demos draw. They are all output of the MIT-licensed generator rather than separate artwork, so licensing them separately would be an odd fiction. Put them in a talk or a paper if they are useful; carry the notice, and a credit line reading “Voltaire, Ashrith Repaka” is welcome though the licence does not demand one.

## Acknowledgements

Voltaire owes three debts worth naming.

**Colormaps.** Every figure on this site draws its colour from perceptually uniform colormap data released into the public domain (CC0): viridis, plasma, and inferno by Stéfan van der Walt and Nathaniel Smith, and cividis by Jamie Nuñez, Christopher Anderton, and Ryan Renslow. The lookup tables are compiled into the site and are the only source of colour in the demos.

**The electrochemistry canon.** The model formulations on the [physics pages](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/physics/) follow two standard references: Newman and Thomas-Alyea, *Electrochemical Systems*, for transport, porous-electrode theory, and concentrated-solution corrections; and Bard and Faulkner, *Electrochemical Methods*, for interfacial kinetics and impedance. Where this site states an equation, the intent is to match these texts, not to improve on them.

**PyBaMM.** The planned cross-validation reference for the porous-electrode (P2D) model. That comparison has not yet been run; its status is tracked honestly on the [validation page](/Electrochemical-Cell-Simulator/validation/).
